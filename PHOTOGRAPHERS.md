## How to use this list

1. **Look before you ask.** Tier 1 frames have been looked at and scored. Tier 2
   frames are real and verified — the place, the time of day and the size come
   from the photo page or its metadata — but nobody has looked at them yet.
   Open each Tier 2 link first and skip anything that isn't beautiful.
2. **One photograph per message.** If you want two from the same person, name
   both.
3. **Ask for the original.** Web copies on Flickr and portfolio sites are often
   1,000–2,000 px. A hero needs 2,400 px or more. The message asks for "the
   largest file you have, without a signature".
4. **Keep the reply.** See "After a yes" at the end.
5. **Mind the live slots.** Direction C is live. Its photo slots are:
   - the cover plate;
   - one golden band;
   - the small back-cover plate, shown as a terracotta duotone;
   - the 404 plate;
   - the transfer page hero;
   - the tour-page place breaks;
   - the route previews.

   The menu backdrop and the closing scene no longer exist. The uses named
   below for "Deep Aegean" (A) and "Cycladic Light" (B) are kept only for the
   record.

The shortlist below (#1–#13) is **all rights reserved** or **CC BY-NC-ND** on
its own page. None of it may be used without the photographer's written
permission, and that is the point of this list.

The one exception is the optional Brecher section at the end: it is CC BY 4.0,
and no permission is needed.

**Contact routes.** Only each platform's own route (Flickr's "Message" button,
an Instagram DM, a public contact page) is used. No personal email addresses
or phone numbers were collected. Flickr mail needs the business's own Flickr
sign-in.

**Which pages may show which frames.**
- The tours name these places: Kourtaliotiko Gorge and the chapel of Saint
  Nicholas, Preveli beach, lagoon and palm forest, Preveli Monastery, and
  Rethymno's town, harbour, Fortezza and old town. A frame of one of them may
  appear on the tour pages. The Heart of Cretan Tradition tour also names
  Sfedoni Cave, the village of Anogeia and the Paraschakis Olive Mill.
- A frame from anywhere else in Crete is **mood only**. It may be used for the
  homepage hero, section bands or the closing scene, with a generic caption.
- The places the copy leaves unnamed stay unnamed: the Kourtaliotis tour's
  "historic village" and "cave-like Byzantine chapel", and the Tradition
  tour's "mountains of central Crete". No frame may be presented as one of
  them.
- **Monuments.** A photographer's permission covers the photograph, not the
  monument in it. Settled on 2026-09-17: frames of the Fortezza, the
  Venetian harbour and lighthouse, or Preveli Monastery go into the Ministry
  licence application (`MONUMENT-LICENCE.md`) as well. That applies to:
  - #3 (also ask the Monastery);
  - #4;
  - #5's harbour frames, and its "The bells of Moni Preveli" (also ask the
    Monastery);
  - #6;
  - #7's old-harbour frame.

## The message — English

**Subject:** Permission to use your photograph "{title}" — Routes Crete

Hello {name},

My name is {sender}. My family and I run Routes Crete, a small private-tour
company based in Rethymno, Crete (www.routescrete.gr). We came across your
photograph "{title}" ({link}) and, honestly, it is one of the most beautiful
images of {place} we have seen.

With your permission, we would love to use it on our website, {where — e.g.
"as the main image on our homepage" or "on our Kourtaliotiko Gorge tour page,
at the Saint Nicholas stop"}.

So you know exactly what we are asking:
- We will credit you by name next to the photograph, and by name with a link
  to your photograph's page on our photo-credits page.
- The image will be colour-graded, and may be resized or cropped, so it sits
  with the look of the site.
- Our website's code is public (on GitHub), so the graded image will be there
  too, just as it is on any website that shows it. Your original file stays
  private with us.
- If you would prefer a fee, we are happy to pay a small one — tell us what
  feels fair.
- If you agree, could you send the largest file you have, without a signature
  or watermark?

All we need is your written confirmation. You can simply reply with this line:

> I grant Routes Crete a non-exclusive, worldwide licence, with no time limit,
> to use my photograph "{title}" on the website routescrete.gr [and on its
> social media], credited to me by name — including the image files the
> website publishes and its public source-code repository; colour grading,
> resizing and cropping are permitted.

(If you would rather it didn't appear on social media, just delete the part in
brackets.)

Thank you so much — for this photograph, and for your pictures.

Kind regards,
{sender}
Routes Crete — Rethymno, Crete
www.routescrete.gr

**Why the line is worded that way** (for the owner, not the photographer):
- It is in writing, and it names the time ("no time limit") and the place
  ("worldwide"). As we understand it, Greek copyright law (ν. 2121/1993)
  expects licences in writing, and it reads a silent contract narrowly.
- The grading clause is there because the site grades every photograph.
- The repository clause is there because the site's code is public.
- This is a practical draft, not legal advice.

## After a yes

1. **Save the reply privately.**
   - An email: save it as a PDF or .eml.
   - An Instagram DM: take a screenshot showing the handle, the date and the
     text, then ask them to confirm by email. A screenshot is weaker evidence
     than an email.
   - Put it in `assets-src/stock-local/permissions/`. That folder is private
     and not in the public repository.
2. **Give us the original file they send.** It goes into the same private
   folder. It is never put in the public repository.
3. **We add a `Written permission` entry** to `content/photo-credits.json`:
   - the photo page, the author exactly as they asked to be named, the date,
     and the scope (website, or website and social media);
   - `permission` pointing at the saved reply;
   - the SHA-1 of the original.

   The credits guard will not let the photograph ship without it.
4. **They are credited** on the page and on `/credits`, as they asked.
